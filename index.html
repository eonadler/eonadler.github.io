Welcome!

I am an Assistant Professor at the University of California, San Diego in the Department of Astronomy & Astrophysics.

My research focuses on galaxy formation, dark matter, and near-field cosmology. A central question my group has been pursuing is whether completely dark (galaxy-free) dark matter halos exist; the answer would reveal how small-scale cosmic structure forms and provide new insight into the nature of dark matter. We tackle these problems using cosmological simulations, models of the galaxy–halo connection, dark matter theory, and observations of dwarf galaxies and strong gravitational lenses.

Building an inclusive scientific community through mentoring, outreach and community service, and teaching is important to me; my efforts in these contexts are described on the corresponding pages.

I am a co-convener of the Dark Energy Science Collaboration (DESC) Dark Matter Working Group and a member of the Satellites Around Galactic Analogs Survey (SAGA) and DECam Local Volume Exploration Survey (DELVE).

Please see my CV, publications, and media for more information!

About me

From 2021 through 2024, I was a joint postdoctoral research fellow at Carnegie Observatories and the University of Southern California. In 2021, I received my Ph.D. in Physics from Stanford University, where I was an NSF Graduate Research Fellow at KIPAC. In 2016, I received my B.S. in Physics from the College of Creative Studies at UC Santa Barbara.

I am passionate about interdisciplinary research. I am currently a Lattimer Research Fellow for Cross-disciplinary Explorations at UC San Diego. Earlier, I co-founded the research collective comp-syn. My interdisciplinary work is described here.
